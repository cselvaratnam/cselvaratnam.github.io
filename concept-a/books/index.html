I am something of a late developer as an author. I found my confidence during my doctoral studies, and my first book was the publication of that research as The Craft of Church Planting. My books and writing come, for the most part, from the intersection of being a practitioner and a theologian — that is my particular approach. I am currently finishing a number of titles I have been working on for a couple of years.

Books

Church Planting Bibliography: Annotated Reading Lists for Students (St Hild Centre for Church Planting, 2026).

A reference book for students of church planting and those who teach them, drawing on more than 25 years of my own study and teaching. It sets out nearly 80 reading lists I have compiled, covering more than 800 annotated books and resources. The book is indexed and cross-referenced. A glossary explains more than 100 terms students meet in the literature, and short biographies introduce more than 40 key authors in the field. If you teach church planting, you are welcome to use it free for non-profit purposes; please do not redistribute it.

Church Planting Bibliography: free copy for teachers

Why Plant Churches? Theological and Practical Reasons (Grove Books, 2023).

Church planting is often reduced to a task of mission. This short book sets out the wider theological and practical reasons for planting, and the benefits it brings to the wider Church.

Why Plant Churches? at Grove Books Why Plant Churches? at Amazon UK

Ian Paul, the editor of Grove Books, interviewed me about this book for Psephizo.

The Craft of Church Planting: Exploring the Lost Wisdom of Apprenticeship (SCM Press, 2022).

Drawing on my doctoral research into the pedagogy of the English medieval craft guilds, this book offers a fresh account of apprenticeship as a model for training in the Church. It argues that church planting, which requires theory and practical skill, is best understood and taught as a craft.

The Craft of Church Planting at SCM Press The Craft of Church Planting at Amazon UK The Craft of Church Planting at Amazon US

Alpha as a Church Planting Tool (Alpha International, 2017).

A short and simple study of why the Alpha course and church planting fit together so naturally. It goes some way to explaining why Alpha has proved such a catalyst for planting.

Download Alpha as a Church Planting Tool (free PDF)

Chapters

‘Is Church Planting a Craft? Training Lessons from Medieval Guilds’, in Joshua Cockayne and Will Foulger (eds), New Churches: A Theology (SCM Press, 2024).

Drawing on the medieval craft guilds, where an apprentice advanced by producing real work rather than by finishing a course, this chapter asks what that older way of forming people might offer the training of church planters now.

New Churches: A Theology at SCM Press New Churches: A Theology at Amazon UK New Churches: A Theology at Amazon US

‘Reclaiming the Missing Generation’, in Winfield Bevins (ed.), Global Voices: Stories of Church Planting from Around the World (Asbury Theological Seminary, 2019).

A case study of G2, the church plant I led in York, which reached the millennial generation largely by apprenticing new leaders.

Global Voices: download (free) Global Voices at Amazon UK Global Voices at Amazon US

In progress

Exploring the Mixed Ecology of Church: How Churches Thrive Together (Grove Books, 2026). to confirm: no public listing found

A primer on the emerging ecclesiology known as the mixed ecology of church, tracing the history of the term and setting out several theological rationales for it. It also identifies key practical questions facing churches and networks.

From Every Nation: Becoming an Intercultural Church in a Multicultural Age (due 2027). to confirm: publisher not stated

Based on research with fifty intercultural church practitioners, this book explores the theological rationale and practical models for intercultural church. In an increasingly multicultural society, most churches now serve a diverse catchment.

Discipleship Revolution: The Wesleys and the Shape of a Methodist Movement (due 2027). to confirm: publisher not stated

An account of the Wesley family and the early British Methodists. It draws out discipleship lessons from the period that can be relearned today.
